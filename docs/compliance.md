# The rules this system checks

`npm run cover -- compliance` (or `/compliance`) runs one check per rule below and cites the rule on every finding. The attention list (`/attention`) uses the same checks, ranked by how much they matter this week.

This is not legal or insurance advice. Each rule records what the law, your policy wording or your contracts expect you to be able to show, with its source. Your own policies set the exact numbers: put them on the policy (`renewal_lead_days`, `notify_within_days`, `average_pct`) or the contractor (`required_pl_cents`) and the checks follow them. If a rule here is out of date for your business, tell Claude Code which rule and what changed; it updates this file and the check together.

| Rule | What it checks | Source | Where the number comes from |
|---|---|---|---|
| `DUTY-OF-DISCLOSURE` | A material change (new site, asset, market, activity, sum insured) that the insurer of its class has not been told about, where that policy renews within its lead time, or where the policy already renewed after the change. The CLI refuses to record a renewal while any change of that class is undisclosed. | Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth), section 21: before a contract is entered into, and before each renewal, the insured must disclose every matter it knows, or a reasonable person would know, is relevant to the insurer's decision. Section 28 sets what the insurer can do when it was not told. In New Zealand, business policies carry the same duty: at common law until the Insurance Contracts Act 2024 is in force, and under that Act's duty of disclosure for non-consumer policies after it. | Each change carries its class. `renewal_lead_days` on the policy (90 in the demo) sets how far ahead the check starts. |
| `RENEWAL-INFORMATION` | A policy ending within 45 days whose renewal information (the proposal or submission to the broker or insurer) has not been recorded as sent. | The same duty: the renewal information is where the disclosure is made. Section 22 of the 1984 Act requires the insurer to tell you about the duty before renewal; meeting it is on you. | Fixed at 45 days; change it with `/customise`. `disclose <policy>` records the date it went. |
| `CONTINUOUS-COVER` | A policy whose period has ended with no renewal, replacement or cancellation recorded. The business may be uninsured for that class today. | Your policy period. Some covers must be held by law or contract (motor third party, public liability in leases and council licences, professional indemnity for some licensed professions). | The policy's `period_end`. |
| `AVERAGE-CLAUSE` | An asset declared below the share of its replacement value set by the policy's average (co-insurance) clause. A claim on it is paid in proportion. | The average or co-insurance clause in your property wording. Section 44 of the Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth) limits average clauses only in home buildings and contents policies, so for business property the clause applies as written. | `average_pct` on the policy (80 in the demo) and the latest `replacement_cents` from `/value`. |
| `VALUATION-AGE` | A building, plant or contents asset with no replacement valuation in the last three years. Building costs move faster than sums insured. | Your broker's and insurer's guidance, and the declared value basis in your wording. Three years is a common cycle; some insurers ask for one every renewal. | Fixed at three years; change it with `/customise`. |
| `CLAIM-NOTIFICATION` | A loss found more than `notify_within_days` ago that the insurer has not been told about, or one told late. | Policy wordings require notice as soon as reasonably practicable. Insurance Contracts Act 1984 (Cth), section 54, limits an insurer to reducing a claim by the prejudice a late notice caused, but that is a dispute worth avoiding. For claims-made covers (professional indemnity, directors and officers, cyber, management liability), section 40(3) lets you notify facts that might give rise to a claim before the policy ends, so they stay covered. | `notify_within_days` on the policy (14 in the demo). |
| `CLAIM-IN-PERIOD` | A claim recorded on an occurrence policy whose loss date falls outside that policy's period. The wrong policy year may have been notified. | Occurrence wordings respond to events within the period of insurance. | The policy's `period_start` and `period_end`. |
| `CLAIM-PROGRESS` | An open, notified claim with no update recorded for 28 days. | The General Insurance Code of Practice 2020 (Australia) commits subscribing insurers to keep you informed of a claim's progress at least every 20 business days. The Fair Insurance Code of the Insurance Council of New Zealand sets the same expectation of regular updates. | Fixed at 28 days; change it with `/customise`. |
| `CONTRACTOR-INSURANCE` | An active contractor with no certificate of currency, an expired one, or a limit below what you require. | Your contracts and site rules. Under the Work Health and Safety Act 2011 (Cth model law), sections 19 and 46, and the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015 (NZ), section 34, you share duties with the contractors on your site; their insurance is how the cost of a loss they cause lands with them, not you. | `required_pl_cents` on each contractor (10 million dollars by default). |

## What this system does not do

- Workers compensation in Australia runs through state schemes, and accident cover in New Zealand through ACC. Neither is a policy you hold here; record the premium or levy as a policy of class `other` if you want it in the spend.
- It does not decide what is material. It keeps the list of changes and refuses to renew until each one has been told to the insurer. The judgement about what to add to the list stays with the business.
- It does not place cover. The broker or insurer does that. This keeps the record the business is accountable for.
